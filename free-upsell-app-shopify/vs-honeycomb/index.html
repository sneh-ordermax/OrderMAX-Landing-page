How they differ in practice

What a "funnel view" actually costs you

Honeycomb's own listing states its free plan includes "100 monthly funnel views," verbatim, as of 26 August 2026. The unit that matters here is view, not order and not dollar. A funnel view is logged the moment a shopper is shown an offer — whether they click it, ignore it, or leave without buying anything at all. A store running a three-step post-purchase funnel can burn three views from a single visitor who buys nothing, while a store with a single cart offer might use one view per checkout. Two stores with identical order volume can exhaust the same 100-view allotment at very different speeds, purely based on how many steps their funnel shows.

OrderMAX has no equivalent counter. There is nothing to watch, no dashboard warning that a plan is about to lapse mid-month, and no incentive to simplify a funnel just to conserve an allowance. The trade-off, covered honestly below, is that "no cap" is not the same claim as "battle-tested at scale" — see the honest trade-off.

Split testing: Honeycomb's real advantage

This is the one place Honeycomb is unambiguously ahead. Automated split testing — running offer variants side by side and measuring which converts — is included on Honeycomb's free tier. That's a feature most competitors in this category, OrderMAX included as of this writing, reserve for a paid plan or don't offer at all. If structured A/B testing of offer copy, placement, or discount depth is a near-term priority rather than a someday feature, that capability being free and immediate on Honeycomb is a legitimate reason to pick it over an app that doesn't have it yet.

One flat plan vs. a metered one

The structural difference isn't features — it's the billing shape. Honeycomb's model is free-then-metered: full functionality gated behind a growing view count, with a steep jump the moment that count is exceeded. OrderMAX's model is flat: one plan, everything included, no second tier waiting on the other side of a threshold. Neither shape is inherently correct; a metered plan lets Honeycomb fund a larger team and support infrastructure off paying customers' fees, while a flat free plan means OrderMAX has no such fee revenue funding it yet — a point worth sitting with before assuming "free forever" is the safer bet by default.

The honest trade-off

Neither app's free plan is a free lunch. Here's what each one actually costs you, stated plainly rather than glossed over:

Honeycomb's real downside: a view-metered free plan expires exactly when it starts working. The moment a store's traffic or offer exposure grows — the exact moment an upsell program starts producing meaningful data — the 100-view allotment gets consumed faster, not slower. A store can do everything right, see its funnel views climb because more shoppers are engaging, and get cut off by the cap precisely because the app is succeeding. The $54.99/mo tier that follows is a roughly 55× jump in views for a fixed fee, the steepest free-to-paid gap of any app we've reviewed in this category — there's no smaller paid step in between.

OrderMAX's real downside: a single Shopify App Store review, against Honeycomb's 146. An uncapped, every-feature-free plan with no paid tier is an unusual structure, and the honest read is that it hasn't yet been tested and reviewed by thousands of merchants the way Honeycomb has. The claims about pricing and features above are accurate as of today — but "accurate" and "proven at scale by public reviews" are two different kinds of confidence, and only one of them exists yet for OrderMAX.

Where OrderMAX fits

OrderMAX fits a store that wants to run upsell offers without a meter to watch — no funnel-view counter, no revenue cap, no calendar countdown to a paid tier. Every feature that exists in the app is available at $0, on the one plan that exists. That is a genuinely different structure from Honeycomb's free-then-metered model, and for a store that doesn't yet need split testing specifically, it removes a category of decision entirely: there's no cap to plan around.

It does not fit a merchant who wants to see the app validated by thousands of public App Store reviews before installing — OrderMAX has one review today, against Honeycomb's 146, and that gap is real. It also does not fit a merchant whose immediate priority is automated split testing, since Honeycomb includes that free right now and OrderMAX does not yet. The honest recommendation is to weigh the metering trade-off against the review-count trade-off for your own store, not to assume either app wins on every axis.

What the evidence says, and where it stops

Tier 2 — named third party, self-reported. AfterSell's aggregate merchant data reports an average 9.74% AOV lift from post-purchase upsell offers specifically. This is vendor-reported aggregate data, not an independent audit — treat it as directional.

Tier 3 — industry benchmark. McKinsey's widely cited analysis attributes roughly 35% of Amazon purchases to recommendation-driven cross-sells. This describes one platform at extreme scale and personalization depth; it is evidence the mechanism works broadly, not a forecast for any specific Shopify store.

What this evidence does not say: neither figure says anything about OrderMAX or Honeycomb specifically, and no independent study has run these two apps head-to-head on matched Shopify stores. The category evidence supports "upsells generally work" — it does not tell you whether a flat free plan or a metered free-to-paid plan captures more of that lift for your store. That comparison only exists if you run it yourself.

Frequently asked questions

Is OrderMAX or Honeycomb better for a Shopify store?

It depends on what you need. OrderMAX gives every feature free with no cap, which suits stores that want to run offers indefinitely without a meter. Honeycomb's free plan includes split testing but caps at 100 monthly funnel views, which suits a low-traffic store that wants A/B testing without paying yet.

What does Honeycomb's free plan actually include?

As of 26 August 2026, Honeycomb's own Shopify App Store listing states its free plan includes 100 monthly funnel views. A funnel view is consumed whenever a shopper sees an offer, whether or not they buy, so the cap can be reached without a single converted sale.

How much does Honeycomb cost after the free plan?

Honeycomb's first paid tier is $54.99/mo for 2,000 monthly funnel views, per its own listing as of 26 August 2026. That is a roughly 55× jump in views for a fixed monthly fee, the steepest free-to-paid gap among the upsell apps we've reviewed in this set.

Does OrderMAX have a paid plan?

No. OrderMAX has one plan: free, with every feature included and no stated cap. There is no paid tier to graduate into and no feature held back for a higher price, which is the core structural difference from Honeycomb's free-then-metered model.

What does Honeycomb do better than OrderMAX?

Split testing. Honeycomb includes automated split testing on its free tier, a feature many competitors — including OrderMAX today — reserve for paid plans or omit entirely. If running structured A/B tests on offer variants matters to you right now, that is a genuine point in Honeycomb's favor.

What is OrderMAX's biggest weakness right now?

Review volume. OrderMAX has a single Shopify App Store review, against competitors like Honeycomb that carry 146. That is a real, disclosed gap in third-party validation — the free, uncapped, every-feature-included plan is accurate, but it hasn't yet been tested by thousands of merchants the way Honeycomb has.

What exactly counts as a "funnel view" on Honeycomb's free plan?

Per Honeycomb's own listing, the free plan is capped by monthly funnel views — an impression of an offer inside a funnel, not a completed purchase. A shopper who sees three upsell steps in one checkout can use more of the 100-view allotment than a shopper who buys once and never sees an offer.

Will Honeycomb's free plan work for a growing store?

Only briefly. Because the cap is metered by views rather than orders or revenue, a free plan's 100 monthly views get consumed faster exactly as traffic and offer exposure increase — meaning the plan tends to run out right as a store's upsell program starts generating meaningful data.

Is a free plan with no paid tier actually sustainable?

That's a fair question to ask any vendor, including OrderMAX. A free, uncapped, single-plan structure has no per-merchant fee funding deeper support infrastructure the way a paid tier's revenue does. It's a real trade-off — merchants should weigh it alongside the disclosed review-count gap before deciding.
