OrderMAX doesn't touch the drawer; it ships every upsell feature free, with no order cap. Which one fits depends on whether your store needs a redesigned cart or durable free access to upsell mechanics.

How they differ in practice

The cart drawer itself, vs. offers layered on top

iCart is the only app in this comparison that rebuilds the cart drawer's UI directly — progress bars toward a spend threshold, free-shipping messaging, and countdown timers are native to the drawer itself, not an overlay bolted onto it. That's a genuinely different category of work than injecting an offer into an existing cart flow, and it's the reason to pick iCart specifically: if your theme's stock cart drawer is the thing you want to redesign, not just supplement.

OrderMAX doesn't rebuild the drawer. It adds upsell offers within your store's existing cart and checkout flow. That's a narrower scope, but it means no cart-UI rebuild risk, and every feature in that scope is included free from day one.

A lifetime allowance vs. no stated cap

iCart's own listing states "First 100 orders free for lifetime" — a one-time allowance across the store's entire order history, not a quota that resets each month. A store doing 25 orders a month burns through it in four months and doesn't get it back; the next order past 100 falls into the $9/mo tier. OrderMAX's free plan has no stated cap at all, so there's no volume threshold to plan around.

An established review base vs. a new listing

iCart carries 434 reviews at a 4.7 average — a large, independently verifiable public track record built over time. OrderMAX has one App Store review. That gap is real and worth weighing on its own, separately from either app's feature set or pricing.

The honest trade-off

iCart's downside: a lifetime-order cap is spent once and doesn't renew. Unlike a monthly cap, which resets the clock every 30 days regardless of how the previous month went, a lifetime allowance counts down permanently from the day the app is installed. A store that front-loads volume — a launch spike, a viral moment — can burn through 100 orders in a week and land in the $9/mo tier long before it has any sense of whether the app is worth paying for.

OrderMAX's downside: a single App Store review. Feature-complete and free is a real offer, but it comes with almost no independent, public evidence of how the app performs across many stores, how support responds under pressure, or what edge cases it hasn't hit yet. iCart's 434 reviews are exactly the kind of evidence OrderMAX doesn't yet have.

Neither trade-off is disqualifying on its own. A store with steady, moderate volume may never feel iCart's lifetime cap as a problem. A store that values feature completeness over track record may be comfortable being an early OrderMAX user. The honest version of this comparison is that both apps are asking you to accept a specific, named risk — not that one app has no downside.

Plainly stated: that comes with a thin public track record. One App Store review is not enough evidence, by itself, to promise how the app performs at scale, and we're not going to pretend otherwise. If a large, independently verifiable review history matters more to your decision than an uncapped free plan, iCart's 434 reviews are a real point in its favor. If the uncapped, full-feature free plan matters more, that's where OrderMAX fits — with that limitation disclosed, not hidden.

Frequently asked questions

Is iCart or OrderMAX better for a Shopify store?

It depends on what you need. iCart is the better pick if you want to replace Shopify's default cart drawer with progress bars, free-shipping thresholds, and countdown timers built in. OrderMAX is the better pick if you want every upsell feature free, permanently, with no order cap or paid tier to graduate into.

What does iCart's free plan actually include?

As of 26 August 2026, iCart's own Shopify App Store listing states "First 100 orders free for lifetime." That is a one-time allowance across the store's entire history, not a monthly quota — once 100 orders have passed through the app, the free plan is spent and the $9/mo tier begins.

Does iCart's free plan renew every month?

No. The cap is lifetime orders, not monthly orders, per iCart's own listing. A store doing 20 orders a month reaches the 100-order ceiling in five months and does not get it back — unlike a monthly-reset cap, a lifetime allowance is used exactly once across the store's history.

What happens after OrderMAX's free plan, if there's no paid tier?

Nothing changes. OrderMAX has no stated order cap and no paid tier to upgrade into — every feature is included at $0 regardless of order volume. The trade-off is a thin track record: a single App Store review, compared to iCart's 434.

Why does iCart replace the cart drawer instead of just adding upsells?

iCart is built around the cart itself rather than around an offer widget — its progress bars, free-shipping thresholds, and countdown timers are native to the drawer UI, not injected on top of it. That is genuinely useful if your theme's stock cart is the thing you actually want to redesign.

Is OrderMAX's single App Store review a red flag?

It is a real limitation worth weighing, not a hidden one. One review means almost no independent, public evidence of how OrderMAX performs across many stores, support responsiveness, or edge cases — compared to iCart's 434 reviews at a 4.7 average. Weigh that against the free, uncapped feature set before deciding.

How much does iCart cost after the free 100 orders?

iCart's first paid tier is $9/mo, covering 101–200 orders, per its own App Store listing. Pricing beyond that tier is not part of the verified facts used on this page — check iCart's current listing directly for tiers above 200 orders before committing a store to it.

Can I use both a cart-drawer app and an upsell app together?

Many merchants do run a cart-redesign app alongside a separate upsell app, but overlapping cart-drawer logic from two apps can conflict or slow the storefront. Test on a duplicate or development store first, and confirm both apps' cart-drawer hooks don't compete for the same UI element.

Which app better suits a low-volume Shopify store?

A low-volume store benefits most from whichever plan doesn't expire while it is still finding its footing. iCart's 100-order lifetime allowance can last a long time at low volume, but it still counts down permanently. OrderMAX's uncapped free plan never counts down at all.

Does a bigger review count mean a better app?

It means more public evidence, not automatically better performance. iCart's 434 reviews at 4.7 stars is a meaningfully larger sample than OrderMAX's single review, and that sample size is a legitimate factor in a decision — but it reflects iCart's longer time on the App Store, not a feature comparison by itself.
