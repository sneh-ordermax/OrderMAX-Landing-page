How they actually differ in practice

The table above is the summary. The mechanics behind it are what actually decide which app fits a given store.

Post-purchase funnel depth

Upsell.com's real edge, per its own listing, is a full-stack post-purchase and thank-you-page funnel — offers that continue after checkout completes, not just at cart or checkout. It also ships A/B testing and advanced analytics identical across every tier, including the free one. OrderMAX does not yet compete on that specific depth; its upsell mechanics center on cart and checkout-adjacent offers rather than a multi-step post-purchase funnel.

What "free" actually runs on

The mechanic difference here is stark, not cosmetic. Upsell.com's free tier, verbatim from its own listing (verified 26 August 2026), is "Available for development stores" — a sandbox for building and testing an offer flow, not a plan that can serve a real customer on a live storefront. OrderMAX's free plan runs on a live store with every feature switched on, with no development-store restriction and no stated order cap.

Where paying starts

Upsell.com's cheapest paid entry point in this comparison is $4.99/mo, starting at 50 orders — the moment a dev-store build goes live and crosses that volume, the fee begins. OrderMAX has no paid tier at all to graduate into; there is no order count or revenue threshold that changes what the app charges, because there is nothing above the free plan.

What each rating actually tells you

2,803 reviews at 4.8 stars is a large, mature sample — it reflects years of merchants running Upsell.com (and ReConvert before the rename) on live stores across a wide range of catalogues and traffic levels. One review, OrderMAX's current count, is not a mature sample by any measure; it is early evidence, not a trend. Neither number changes what either app's free plan does today, but they answer different questions: Upsell.com's rating speaks to how the app has held up over time and at scale, while OrderMAX's free plan speaks to what a merchant can try right now, on a live store, without a track record to lean on yet.

The honest trade-off, for both

Neither app's free plan is a free lunch. Each one "spends" its free tier differently, and each has a real cost worth naming plainly rather than glossing over.

Upsell.com's real downside Its free tier isn't usable on a live storefront at all. A merchant who wants Upsell.com's post-purchase depth without paying has to build and test on a development store, then start paying the moment that store goes live and crosses 50 orders. Free, in practice, buys evaluation time — not a running app on real customers.
OrderMAX's real downside It's free everywhere, including on a live store, with no cap disclosed — but it carries a single App Store review, next to a competitor with 2,803. That's not a hidden catch; it's an honest gap in track record that a longer feature list hasn't yet closed, and it should factor into the decision directly.

Where OrderMAX fits

OrderMAX fits a store that wants to run real upsell offers on a live storefront today, at zero cost, without an order cap forcing an upgrade decision down the line. It does not yet fit a store that specifically needs Upsell.com's deeper post-purchase funnel logic, its built-in A/B testing at that depth, or the reassurance of a large, multi-year review history — OrderMAX currently has one review on the Shopify App Store, against Upsell.com's 2,803.

That gap is worth treating as a real input to the decision, not a footnote to work around. A newer app with a thin review history carries a different kind of risk than a capped or development-store-only free plan — neither is automatically the safer choice, and both are worth weighing against what a specific store actually needs on day one.

Practically, that means a store already running well-established post-purchase upsell logic, and specifically wanting Upsell.com's thank-you-page funnels or its built-in A/B testing, has little reason to switch on the strength of this page alone. A store that hasn't started upselling yet, or that has been putting it off because every option seemed to come with a cap, a card requirement, or a countdown, is the clearer fit — install on a live store, see what the mechanics do on real orders, and treat the single-review track record as a known, disclosed trade for that access.

What the evidence says, and where it stops

Tier 2 — named third party, self-reported. AfterSell's aggregate merchant data reports an average 9.74% AOV lift from post-purchase upsell offers specifically. This is vendor-reported aggregate data, not an independent audit — treat it as directional.

Tier 3 — industry benchmark. McKinsey's widely cited analysis attributes roughly 35% of Amazon purchases to recommendation-driven cross-sells. This describes one platform at extreme scale and personalization depth; it is evidence the mechanism works broadly, not a forecast for any specific Shopify store.

What this evidence does not say: neither figure tells you whether OrderMAX or Upsell.com captures more of that lift for a specific catalogue, and no independent study has run the two apps head-to-head on matched Shopify stores. The category evidence supports "upsells generally work broadly" — it does not support "more reviews means more lift," and it does not support "a deeper post-purchase funnel always outperforms a simpler one." That gap is exactly why the honest trade-offs above, not the review count alone, should decide which app fits a given store.
