BOGOS: Free Gift Bundle Upsell vs Honeycomb Upsell & Cross Sell

Two well-reviewed Shopify apps, two completely different free-plan currencies. One counts your orders, once, for the life of the store. The other counts every visitor who so much as glances at an offer, reset monthly. That single difference — not the feature lists — decides which free tier actually survives contact with real traffic.

Neither app wins outright. BOGOS is the deeper gifting and bundling engine, with a one-time 30-order lifetime free cap; Honeycomb is a funnel-builder that includes split testing free, capped at 100 monthly funnel views — a limit that burns on lookers, not just buyers. The right pick depends on your mechanic and your traffic shape.

How they differ in practice

The pricing tables above tell you what each plan costs. They don't tell you what the two apps are actually built to do — and on that, BOGOS and Honeycomb aren't really competing for the same job.

Promotional depth vs. funnel-building with testing

BOGOS's specialty, per its own listing, is gift-with-purchase, buy-one-get-one, bundles, and mix-and-match offers — promotional mechanics built specifically for gifting-style incentives. Honeycomb is built around upsell funnels instead, with split testing included even on its free tier — a feature most competitors in this category, BOGOS included, reserve for paid plans. A store running "spend $75, get a free gift" needs BOGOS's mechanic; a store trying to A/B test which post-purchase offer converts better needs Honeycomb's.

Twelve years and 95,000+ brands vs. a newer, testing-first entrant

BOGOS (formerly Free Gifts by Secomapp) has spent twelve years specifically in gift-and-bundle promotions, now used by more than 95,000 brands — a long runway for edge cases in that one mechanic to get resolved, reflected in its 4,092-review history. Honeycomb, by Conversion Bear, has a smaller review base at 146, but ships a genuinely differentiated feature for that stage: split testing that doesn't require a paid plan to access.

Sales channel reach: POS and headless vs. storefront funnels

BOGOS's listing specifically calls out POS and headless storefront support — relevant to any brand selling in person or through a custom-built front end, where a purely storefront-embedded funnel builder won't render at all. Honeycomb's strength is concentrated in the standard online storefront funnel: cart, product page, and post-purchase steps it can test variants of directly. Neither claim covers the same ground, which is why they aren't a strict apples-to-apples swap.

The honest trade-off

Neither free plan is free of a real cost. Both vendors have simply chosen to charge it in a different currency than dollars.

BOGOS's real downside: the free plan spends itself once, permanently

A lifetime cap is a one-time budget, not a monthly allowance. Thirty orders is roughly a single week for a moderately active store — burn through it, and there is no reset next month, no annual refresh, nothing. Once BOGOS's free tier is used, the only way back to using the app at all is a paid plan starting at $29.99/mo. Compared to a plan that renews indefinitely, "free" here behaves closer to a one-time trial with an unusually generous name.

Honeycomb's real downside: the cap expires exactly when the app starts working

Honeycomb's free plan counts views, not orders — anyone who lands on a funnel offer consumes part of the 100-view monthly allowance, whether or not they buy anything. That means the cap is most likely to run out precisely when a funnel is doing its job and getting seen by more shoppers, not when it's failing. And the jump to the next tier is steep: $54.99/mo for 2,000 views is a 55x increase over the free allowance, the largest free-to-paid gap of any app compared on this site so far.

What the evidence says, and where it stops

Tier 2 — named third party, self-reported. AfterSell's aggregate merchant data reports an average 9.74% AOV lift from post-purchase upsell offers specifically. This is vendor-reported aggregate data, not an independent audit — treat it as directional.

Tier 3 — industry benchmark. McKinsey's widely cited analysis attributes roughly 35% of Amazon purchases to recommendation-driven cross-sells. This describes one platform at extreme scale and personalization depth; it is evidence the mechanism works broadly, not a forecast for any specific Shopify store.

What this evidence does not say: neither figure was measured on BOGOS or Honeycomb specifically, and no independent study has run the two apps head-to-head on matched Shopify stores. The category evidence supports "upsell and gifting mechanics generally lift AOV" — it says nothing about which of these two apps lifts it more for your catalogue. That gap is exactly why the free plan of whichever app fits your use case is worth testing before either store pays for anything.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better, BOGOS or Honeycomb?

Neither wins outright — they're built for different jobs. BOGOS is the deeper choice for gift-with-purchase, BOGO, and bundle promotions, with twelve years of track record behind it. Honeycomb is a funnel-builder with split testing included on its free tier, a feature most competitors reserve for paid plans. The right pick depends on the mechanic you actually need.

What's the difference between BOGOS's and Honeycomb's free plans?

BOGOS's free plan is 30 orders total, for the lifetime of the store — a one-time allowance that never renews once used. Honeycomb's free plan is 100 funnel views per month, and it resets every month, but a "view" is counted for anyone who merely sees an offer, not just people who buy something.

Does BOGOS's free plan renew every month?

No. As of 26 August 2026, BOGOS's own App Store listing describes its free plan as "30 free orders lifetime," a one-time allowance for the life of the store, not a monthly quota. Once those 30 orders are used, the free plan is exhausted permanently, with no monthly reset.

Does Honeycomb's free cap count actual sales or just views?

Just views. As of 26 August 2026, Honeycomb's own listing states "100 monthly funnel views" as its free allowance — consumed by anyone who lands on a funnel offer, whether or not they buy anything. A store with real traffic but low conversion can burn through the cap fast with little to show for it.

How many reviews do BOGOS and Honeycomb have?

As of 26 August 2026, BOGOS: Free Gift Bundle Upsell shows 5.0 stars across 4,092 reviews on the Shopify App Store. Honeycomb Upsell & Cross Sell shows 4.6 stars across 146 reviews. BOGOS carries a substantially larger and longer review history in this category.

What does BOGOS cost after the free plan?

BOGOS's paid tiers, per its own listing, are $29.99/mo for the Basic plan (300 orders), $49.99/mo for Grow (600 orders), and $109.99/mo for Plus (2,000 orders), plus a $0.05 charge per order beyond your plan's cap. Each tier buys order-volume headroom, not additional features you're missing on Basic.

What does Honeycomb cost after the free plan?

Honeycomb's first paid tier is $54.99/mo for 2,000 monthly funnel views, per its own listing. That's a 55x jump from the free tier's 100 views — the steepest free-to-paid jump among the apps compared on this site so far. Honeycomb's listing does not break out further named tiers beyond that starting price.

Which app is better for gift-with-purchase or BOGO promotions?

BOGOS. Gift-with-purchase, buy-one-get-one, bundles, and mix-and-match are its core specialty, refined over twelve years and used by more than 95,000 brands. Honeycomb is built around upsell funnels, not promotional gifting mechanics, so it isn't the natural fit for a GWP-style offer.

Which app is better for split testing without paying for it?

Honeycomb. Split testing is included on its free tier, per its own listing — a feature most competitors in this category, BOGOS included, reserve for paid plans. If testing offer variants matters more to you than gifting mechanics, that free inclusion is a genuine reason to pick Honeycomb.
