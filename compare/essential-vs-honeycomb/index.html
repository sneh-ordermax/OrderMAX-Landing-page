Honeycomb Upsell & Cross Sell vs Essential Upsell & Cross Sell

Both apps give you a genuine free plan. What ends it is completely different: one counts how many people saw your offer, the other counts how much money it actually made you. That single difference decides which one fits a given store far more than either app's marketing page will tell you.

The short answer

Essential Upsell & Cross Sell's free plan never expires until the app has generated up to $100 in attributed revenue, while Honeycomb's free plan expires after 100 monthly funnel views regardless of sales. Essential also carries far more reviews (2,234 vs. 146) at a perfect 5.0 rating. Honeycomb's genuine edge is split testing included free, a feature Essential doesn't offer at $0.

How they differ in practice

The cap unit changes what "running out" means

Honeycomb's free plan is metered in funnel views — every time a shopper sees an offer, whether or not they buy anything, it counts against the 100-view monthly limit. Essential's free plan is metered in attributed revenue — nothing counts against the $100 limit until the app has actually generated a sale. Same word, "free," two entirely different clocks.

Split testing is a real free-tier edge for Honeycomb

Per its own listing, Honeycomb includes split testing on its free plan — a feature most competitors in this category, Essential included, hold back for paid tiers. If the priority is running structured A/B tests on offer copy or placement before spending anything, Honeycomb's free plan supports that workflow directly; Essential's free-tier listing doesn't advertise the equivalent.

The 55× jump vs. the gradual climb

Honeycomb's first paid tier is $54.99/mo — a 55× jump from its $0 plan, the steepest free-to-paid jump between these two apps. Essential's first paid tier is $9.99/mo, then scales up gradually to $500/mo at its higher tiers. A store outgrowing its free plan faces a very different first invoice depending on which app it picked.

Review volume as a maturity signal

Essential carries 2,234 reviews at a 5.0 rating; Honeycomb carries 146 at 4.6. Review count isn't a feature and shouldn't be read as one, but it is a rough proxy for how many merchants have run the app long enough, across enough edge cases, to leave feedback — worth weighing alongside the cap and pricing differences above.

The honest trade-off in each

Honeycomb's downside: a view-based cap is consumed by anyone who merely looks, not just anyone who buys. A store with heavy traffic and a low conversion rate can burn through 100 monthly funnel views from browsers who never purchase, hitting the cap and getting pushed toward the $54.99/mo tier without the free plan ever having driven a single extra sale. High-traffic, low-conversion stores are the ones most exposed to this.

Essential's downside: a revenue-based cap expires exactly when it starts working. The free plan is only ever at risk of running out once the app has proven itself by generating up to $100 in attributed revenue — which means the moment a merchant has evidence the app is worth using is also the moment the free tier is closest to ending. It's a gentler trade-off than Honeycomb's, but it's still a trade-off, not a free pass.

Neither app's free tier is a trick. Both simply meter usage on a different axis, and each axis penalizes a different kind of store.

Frequently asked questions

What's the main difference between Honeycomb and Essential's free plans?

The unit that runs out. Honeycomb's free plan, per its own App Store listing, allows "100 monthly funnel views" — consumed by anyone who sees an offer. Essential's free plan allows "up to $100 additional revenue generated" — consumed only by results the app actually produces.

Which app has the cheaper first paid plan?

Essential, by a wide margin. Its first paid tier starts at $9.99/mo, scaling up to $500/mo at higher tiers. Honeycomb's first paid tier is $54.99/mo for 2,000 views — a 55× jump from its $0 tier, the steepest free-to-paid jump between these two.

Does Honeycomb offer split testing on its free plan?

Yes. Split testing is included on Honeycomb's free tier, per its own listing — a feature most competitors in this category, including Essential's listing, reserve for paid plans. It's the clearest feature edge Honeycomb has over Essential at $0.

Can a free plan's view cap run out before it makes any sales?

Yes, with Honeycomb specifically, because its cap counts views, not purchases. A store with heavy traffic and a low conversion rate can burn through 100 monthly funnel views from browsers who never buy, hitting the cap without the app ever driving a single extra sale.

Does Essential's free plan ever run out if the app isn't working?

No — and that's the trade-off in the other direction. Essential's free plan is capped by attributed revenue, so a store where the app isn't generating sales never hits the cap and never gets pushed to upgrade. It only expires once the app has demonstrably worked.

Which app has more Shopify App Store reviews?

Essential Upsell & Cross Sell, with 2,234 reviews at a 5.0 rating, compared to Honeycomb's 146 reviews at 4.6. Review volume isn't a feature comparison, but it's a real signal of how many merchants have used each app long enough to leave one.

Is a revenue-capped free plan or a view-capped free plan better for a new store?

For a brand-new store with unproven traffic, a revenue cap (Essential) is generally lower-risk, since it can't run out before the app has produced results. A view cap (Honeycomb) suits a store that already has steady traffic and wants split testing included from day one.

Do either of these apps offer unlimited free upsells?

No. Both Honeycomb and Essential cap their free plans — Honeycomb at 100 monthly funnel views, Essential at $100 in additional attributed revenue. Neither listing advertises an uncapped free tier; both are free until a specific, different kind of usage limit is reached.
